How Much Safer

No study compares breach outcomes for serverless and server hosting. The attack-surface literature (Manadhata and Wing, IEEE TSE 2011, and Howard's relative attack surface quotient) defines how to count entry points and capabilities, and neither measures this pair. The vendor surveys searched quote percentages that were read only through summaries, so none is repeated here.

What can be counted is the two tables above. Every sentence on this page that says Drupflare is more resistant than a VPS to a class is Inferred from the architecture until the three-arm comparison below has a result.

The 24 rows of the incident map carry these verdicts:

Verdict Rows Meaning
removed 12 The attack path needs a component the object lacks
contained 4 The flaw runs; the sandbox limits the reach
open 8 The flaw works as it does anywhere, or it could not be established either way

The v1.0.4 class table has 19 rows: 4 move from open to removed, 13 to contained, and 2 stay open. Read the removed counts as statements about classes of server takeover and published exploit chains, and do not read them as a breach probability. The incident rows were chosen from incidents that reached the attacker's goal on conventional PHP hosting, so the selection favours the classes this architecture removes. None of the six Drupal advisories in the map is removed: each is contained or open, because Drupal's own bugs run inside the sandbox. Sophos's 2025 education survey reports that higher education providers were more likely to be attacked through exploited vulnerabilities (35%), from 441 respondents across lower and higher education (Sophos). The figure concerns the entry vector and says nothing about the host.

The measurable form of the claim is a comparison of authority retained after the same exploit. The planned experiment runs one pinned vulnerable Drupal against three arms (a minimally configured VPS, a hardened container and Drupflare) with a fixed list of post-exploitation actions, and publishes how many each arm blocked.
