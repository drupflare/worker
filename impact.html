Impact

What Drupflare costs to run in electricity, carbon, water and money, and how far each figure can be trusted. The figures are split across four pages:

  • Energy: the saving against conventional hosting, the operating states, and the per-request model.
  • Energy Per View: the energy of one view, the traffic mix, visitor devices and Spool.
  • Fleet Energy: many sites at once, institutional workloads and the saving by site count.
  • Environment: carbon, water and embodied carbon.
  • Economics: what each host costs at each traffic level.

This page holds what they share: the labels, the limits of every claim, how to reproduce the numbers, and the numbered references the other pages cite.

Measurement Labels

Every figure on this page carries one of four labels.

label meaning
measured read off an instrument on real hardware, with an n and a spread
derived arithmetic over measured inputs and published constants
derived (modelled) arithmetic that also takes an input nobody has measured for a real site
published quoted from a named external source, cited where it appears

A figure keeps its label wherever it is repeated.

What This Does Not Claim

  • No per-tenant Cloudflare wattage. It is not observable. Probing a deployed Cloudflare Container found no energy counter by any of the six routes a Linux process has, and no energy field exists anywhere in the account's analytics schema. The Cloudflare side of every figure above is an allocation over measured workload and published hardware.
  • No cost for custom domains. The bills above are Workers account bills. A site on its own domain through Cloudflare for SaaS adds $0.10 a hostname a month past the first 100 [40], so 1,000 of them add $90 a month and take the asleep bill at 10,000 views from $5.80 to $95.80. How a fleet's domains attach decides whether that applies, and scripts/economics/pricing.ts takes the count.
  • No consolidated-host bill. The conventional bills price one origin a site. A consolidated host pays for memory alone at least $5.00 a GiB a month at the cheapest sourced plan: $5,000 for 1,000 sites at 1 GiB each, $1,250 at 0.25 GiB and $500 at 0.1 GiB, before CPU, disk, transfer and backups. A bill of $350.65 buys 71.8 MiB of memory a site across 1,000 sites and $5.80 buys 1.2 MiB, so a consolidated host undercuts the kept-warm bill only if a site fits in about 72 MiB. No source gives a Drupal site's memory on a shared host.
  • No claim that 1,000 sites replace 1,000 servers. VPS providers and managed platforms already consolidate, so the comparison is against hosting that has captured most of the multi-tenancy saving.
  • Conventional prices are sized list prices. They exclude labour, a CDN, the database and file replication of a multi-region origin and cross-region transfer, and they price a box from documented minimums and a measured stack. The stack was measured with SQLite and not MariaDB, on one machine and on a Cloudflare container, and no source gives PHP-FPM memory per child for Drupal.
  • The energy worst case and the priced box differ in size. The energy model charges a VPS a 320th of a host, two vCPUs. The cost model prices one vCPU and 2 GiB, which is a 192nd of a 384 GiB host by memory, so the energy charge is at most low for the box priced.
  • Pantheon as a price, not an energy baseline. It is already multi-tenant, and how many machines it runs, how well utilised they are and how much of that would persist without a given workload are all unobservable from outside.
  • No market-share multiplier. A CMS's share of websites is a count of sites and not a quantity of compute. It changes the addressable population and transfers no measurement.
  • Incremental energy is not a hosting total. The rig produces idle-subtracted workload energy. A continuously provisioned server also pays its idle, and the equivalent allocation on a shared edge fleet cannot be measured. Operating States brackets it from zero to a memory-share bound; neither end is a reading.
  • No allocation for Cloudflare's own fleet utilisation. The 2.22 W a busy core is the full-load floor, and Watts For A Busy Core shows what a lower host load would charge. At 10,000 views that leaves the saving at 99.98% against one VPS, and it moves no per-view ratio, since both sides use the same figure.
  • No cold-encounter reading. The headline carries none, and no deployed site has reported coldOfTraffic. The sensitivity rows are assumptions about how many editor sessions find an object cold.
  • No price on the hostname lookup. A request through the production hostname resolves the site from a key-value mapping, memoised for a minute, and its CPU time has not been read. Its wall time is 4 ms warm and 46 to 140 ms in a colo that has not seen the key, and CPU time excludes the wait. The closest per-view cell has room for about 0.3 ms of extra CPU on every view against a free hit and 0.9 ms against an nginx hit, at a million views a month and above.
  • No price on the shell tier, replica lanes or the plan's compile. A logged-in view that misses the compiled plan can be answered from a stored shell, which renders a fragment instead of the page. Lanes copy writes to every object of a busy site. The plan compiles from three agreeing sessions. None has an energy class here, and How The Lead Moves charges a missed plan as a render.
  • No visitor-device energy in any total above. Visitor Devices prices a repeat navigation on its own. It is a desktop reading with the fresh-asset share swept, no phone has been measured, and it is never summed with the server figures.
  • No price on the v1.0.4 security controls. The CPU readings above predate the PHP 8.5.12 interpreter and the controls, and no deployed cpuTime has been read since. What is counted: a new object's first fill writes one more row, 83 against 82, once per object for the module integrity record (measured). The interpreter is 14,011,458 bytes against 13,401,742, 4.5% larger (measured). An owner action appends to the audit log, and no view triggers one. A claimed site hashes each module file the first time it opens it. Heap image checks run only where HEAP_IMAGE is on, which is not the default. The report-only policy header adds about 120 bytes to each HTML response before compression (derived). None of these has an energy figure.
  • Institutional rows use an assumed workload. The page views come from the case studies. The logged-in share, the saves a day and the pages a save invalidates are the model's, not theirs.

Reproducing The Numbers

bun scripts/measure/vps-energy.ts run --ssh="$HOST" --target="$VPS_ADDR" --target2="$DRUPFLARE_ADDR" --ladder=2,4,8 --n=5
bun scripts/measure/render-fraction.ts
bun scripts/economics/perview.ts
bun scripts/economics/states.ts
bun scripts/economics/device.ts
bun scripts/economics/fleet.ts
bun scripts/economics/comparison.ts --md
bun scripts/economics/comparison.ts --crossover
bun scripts/economics/comparison.ts --sites=426 --views=82160

The first command needs a bare-metal x86 host with readable RAPL counters and a load generator on a different machine. A virtual machine cannot substitute: a Cloudflare Container exposes no energy counter at all, and a mainstream KVM guest exposes a power PMU that reads a constant zero, which a tool reports as 0 W. --ladder is required for a per-request figure. One rate cannot separate the cost of a request from the cost of the package being awake, so the two arms are read at the same request count.

Conventional host prices live in scripts/economics/hosting-catalog.json: every plan and rate carries the provider page it was read from and the line quoted from it, and tests/node/hosting-catalog.spec.ts fails on one without a source. The economic models live in scripts/economics/. Every measured input they take is in one file with its provenance and the workload it was taken under, because a render cost measured with cold caches and a render cost measured on the invalidation path differ by 35x and are not substitutable.
