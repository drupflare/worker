Energy

How much electricity a Drupflare site uses against the hosting it replaces, and where the saving comes from. Energy Per View and Fleet Energy extend the model; Environment turns the totals into carbon and water, and Economics prices them. Every figure carries a label defined on Impact. Numbered citations such as [1] refer to the References list on Impact.

Energy Savings

Total energy of one site over a year, idle included, for drupflare and for a production deployment. A conventional deployment draws power whether or not anyone visits; drupflare's figure is the CPU time its views use, logged-in views included. Every cell is derived (modelled): no source sizes a production node, so the production shapes are modelled, and drupflare's energy is derived from measured CPU time on the traffic mix. A month is a twelfth of each figure. Generated by bun scripts/economics/perview.ts.

This is the sleeping state, a site that holds nothing between requests. A site kept warm, or one that wakes cold, draws more. Operating States prices both, and the saving against production stays above 99.5% in every state.

Production is three regions with a high-availability web pair and database pair in each, sized at 15% utilisation. Peak-sized is the same three regions with 8-vCPU nodes at 10%, 390 kWh a year. The multiple is the ratio of the two totals. It grows as traffic falls because drupflare's total has no idle term, so quote the percentage.

Every figure is rounded down, so each is a floor. A server is the share of one 640-thread host the model assigns to the deployment: 12 nodes at 4 or 8 vCPU each, counted in whole servers and, below one, as the largest unit fraction that fits, derived (modelled). The host is the SPECpower result for a Dell PowerEdge R6725 with two AMD EPYC 9845 chips, 320 cores and 640 hardware threads, which idles at 135 W and draws 711 W at 100% load [1]. A US home is the EIA's 2022 average of 10,791 kWh a year [2], and a passenger vehicle emits about 4.6 t of CO2 a year [3].
